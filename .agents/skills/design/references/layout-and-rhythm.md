# Layout Architecture and Spatial Rhythm

Layout translates cognitive priorities into visual reading order, grouping, hierarchy, and breathing room.

## 1. Spatial Rhythm Principles

- Proximity before Borders: Place related items close together; separate distinct groups with generous whitespace before introducing container borders or colored backgrounds.
- Spacing Scale: Use a consistent spacing progression (such as 4pt, 8pt, 12pt, 16pt, 24pt, 32pt, 48pt) rather than arbitrary random gaps.
- Asymmetry in Vertical Rhythm: Place more whitespace above a section heading (e.g. 24-32pt) than below it (e.g. 8-12pt) to visually bind the heading to its succeeding content block.
- Container Inset Floor: Always provide at least 8pt to 12pt of padding inside bordered or colored containers. Never place text flush against container edges.
- Margin Inset Floor: Never let text or visual elements touch the outer boundary of the slide (minimum 16pt to 24pt margin gutters).
- Edge-Flush Discipline: Avoid full-width container cards spanning edge-to-edge without gutters unless purposefully creating full-bleed hero banners.

## 2. Multi-Column Balance Rule

- Balanced Column Heights: In two- or three-column slides, ensure content heights are balanced so one column does not run down the entire page while another floats in empty space.
- Flow Long Copy Below: If one section has substantial explanatory copy, place the primary diagram or table in an upper row and flow the text in a full-width block below.
- Column Ratio Rules: Use deliberate split ratios (50/50 for comparisons, 60/40 for hero visual with notes, 33/33/33 for three-phase pipelines).

## 3. Common Layout Formats in Slides

- 1:1 Symmetric Split:
  - Left column: Diagram, architecture figure, or equation block.
  - Right column: Explanatory observations and takeaways.
- 3:2 Asymmetric Split:
  - 60% prominent visual element (neural network architecture or comparison plot).
  - 40% concise telegraphic bullet analysis.
- 3-Column Metric or Pipeline Tiles:
  - Three distinct comparative models, experimental conditions, or sequence steps side-by-side.
- Hero Focus with Peripheral Notes:
  - Central diagram or formula prominently centered with annotations placed near connection points.

## 4. Whitespace as an Active Tool

- Do not attempt to fill every corner of a 16:9 canvas.
- Unfilled margin areas reduce cognitive strain, guide eye movement directly to the central exhibit, and prevent visual fatigue during lengthy presentations.
