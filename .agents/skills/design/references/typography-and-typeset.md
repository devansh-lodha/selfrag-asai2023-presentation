# Typography and Typesetting Standards

Typography establishes reading order, informational hierarchy, mathematical precision, and voice.

## 1. Typographic Hierarchy for Slides

Maintain a consistent typographic scale across all slides with at least a 1.25x ratio between adjacent hierarchical steps:

- Section Title / Chapter Header (Level 1): 28pt to 34pt, bold
- Slide Title / Action Title (Level 2): 22pt to 26pt, bold / semibold
- In-Slide Subheading / Section Card Header (Level 3): 18pt to 20pt, semibold
- Body Text / Main Bullets: 16pt to 20pt, regular
- Secondary Text / Sub-bullets: 14pt to 16pt, regular / muted
- Captions, Footnotes, Source Attribution: 11pt to 13pt, muted
- Mathematical Formulas (Display): Match adjacent body or title scale proportionally

## 2. Line Length, Measure, and Leading

- Reading Measure: Keep body text lines between 45 and 75 characters wide for comfortable tracking. Lines wider than 80 characters cause tracking fatigue.
- Slide Text Blocks: Prefer compact, column-constrained text blocks rather than full-width spans across a 16:9 canvas.
- Line Height (Leading):
  - Headings (22pt+): 1.15 to 1.30x font size.
  - Body Text (16-20pt): 1.40 to 1.60x font size. Tight leading below 1.35x impairs multi-line reading speed.
  - Wider lines require proportionally more leading.

## 3. Letter Spacing (Tracking)

- Headings and Display: Tighten tracking slightly (-0.01em to -0.02em) for optical cohesion in larger glyphs. Never crush tracking destructively below -0.04em.
- Body Text: Standard tracking (0.0em). Never over-track body text above +0.05em.
- All-Caps Badges and Short Labels: Wide tracking (+0.05em to +0.10em) is acceptable only on short uppercase labels, abbreviations, or acronyms.

## 4. Font Family Pairings

Use at most two font families across an entire presentation:
- Primary Presentation Sans-Serif: High legibility at distance and presentation scale (such as Inter, Roboto, Fira Sans, Helvetica Neue).
- Math and Formal Prose: Clean serif or native math font (such as New Computer Modern, Linux Libertine).
- Code, Tensors, and Architecture Labels: Fixed-width monospace (such as Fira Code, DejaVu Sans Mono, JetBrains Mono).
- Tabular Figures: Enable tabular figures (lining numbers with equal widths) in tables, performance matrices, and metric readouts.

## 5. Optical Compensation on Dark vs. Light Surfaces

- Light Text on Dark Backgrounds: Appears optically bolder and slightly tighter due to light halation. Compensate with slightly lighter weight, a touch wider tracking (+0.01em), and slightly increased line height.
- Dark Text on Light Backgrounds: Provides higher natural contrast; use standard weight and tight tracking for headings.

## 6. Text Discipline and Alignment

- Use bold sparingly for key terms on first introduction and primary empirical findings.
- Use italic for mathematical variables, foreign phrases, and paper publication titles.
- Use left alignment for body text. Avoid fully justified text without hyphenation.
- Never use all-caps for long sentences or paragraphs.
- Keep punctuation within quotes when matching standard academic conventions.
